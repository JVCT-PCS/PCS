What Is This Tool?
The Civilian Training Task List is an interactive, browser-based training management guide designed specifically for Department of the Air Force civilian employees in the Transportation Clerk & Assistant (GS-2102) and Traffic Management Specialist (GS-2130) series. It covers the full career spectrum from GS-05 through GS-15 and is focused on Personal Property operations, Manning management, and Operational Guidance.

The tool consolidates regulatory references, task checklists, onboarding steps, career progression frameworks, and trainer guidance into a single document — eliminating the need to search across multiple publications to manage a training program.
Key publications integrated into this tool: DAFI 24-602V4 (2026) · DTR Part IV Chapters A-401 through A-413 · JTR · OCONUS PPCIG (Sept 2025) · OPM GS-2102 & GS-2130 Standards · DoW PPA Business Rules · DTR Appendix A-M Forms

Navigating the Tabs
Onboarding
Start here on your first day. Contains five sequential steps covering HR actions (benefits, PD review, IDP), Federal ID card and CAC/PIV setup, building access, computer/network access, and all mandatory federal training (Cyber Awareness, CUI, CTIP, Antiterrorism, EEO, Ethics). Work through Steps 1–5 in order during your first 30 days.
Development Framework
Review after completing onboarding. Contains two resources: (1) the DAF Foundational Competency Model — four domains (Developing Self, Developing Others, Developing Organizations, Developing Ideas) showing the competencies expected of all civilians; and (2) the GS Career Progression Pyramid — a visual roadmap of training, education, and development from GS-05 entry level through GS-15 senior leader. Use this to understand where you are and where you are going.
Apprentice / Entry GS-05–07
Contains six sub-sections: Regulatory Guidance (with full DTR Chapter Index), Personal Property Forms (all 33 forms from DTR App A-M), Personal Property Operations (counseling, shipment management outbound/inbound, storage SIT/NTS, QA foundational, PPM, specialized shipments, BLUEBARK/Safety Moves), and Manning & Operational Guidance. This is the starting point for all task certification at entry level.
Intermediate GS-07–09
Builds on apprentice content. Covers complex counseling, CONUS/OCONUS exceptions, DPM shipments, independent QA inspections, PPM GCC calculation, full specialized move processing (POV, firearms, boats, mobile homes, specialized), BLUEBARK and Safety Move processing, and section-level manning management.
Advanced GS-11–12
Focuses on program management. Covers section-level regulatory authority, SOP development, QA program management (residential/warehouse inspections, TSP punitive actions, invoice reconciliation, carrier violation reporting), and section-level manning (MTL/MTP, duty schedules, PD revisions).
Expert GS-13–15
Enterprise-level responsibilities. Covers publication change recommendations, SME representation at DAF PPA HQ working groups, QCI training program development, command-level TSP oversight, strategic manning, and career field training feedback.
Trainer Guidance
Four-phase training management framework for supervisors and certified trainers: (1) Planning the Training Program, (2) Conducting OJT using the four-step method, (3) Training Documentation & Certification, and (4) Program Evaluation & Continuous Improvement. Use this section to manage every trainee in your section.
5
Using the Tier Blocks
Each section contains multiple Tier Blocks — collapsible panels that group related tasks. Each block has a colored badge identifying its category (e.g., Regulatory Guidance, Personal Property, Manning & Ops, Forms).
  • Click the block header to expand or collapse it. The arrow (▾) rotates when expanded. Blocks default to open/closed based on typical workflow order.
  • Read the badge color to quickly identify content type. Blue = Apprentice. Teal = Intermediate. Amber = Advanced. Purple = Expert. Green = Onboarding. Violet = Forms.
  • Review the Task Group title (gray uppercase header inside the block) to understand which functional area the tasks below belong to (e.g., "Shipment Management — Outbound," "Quality Assurance," "Storage — SIT and NTS").
  • Read the task title and description for each item. The description provides procedural context, regulatory authority, and instructions for completing the task.
  • Check for orange Trainer Action boxes at the bottom of some tasks. These provide specific instructions to supervisors and trainers on how to teach and certify that task.
Important — Tier Blocks do not auto-save state: The tool runs entirely in your browser. If you close or refresh the page, checkbox completions will reset. Use the Download as HTML button to save a local copy that preserves all content. Track official task completions in your agency's training management system (e.g., training record folder / DAF Form 623 equivalent).

Checking Off Tasks
Each task item has a checkbox on the left side. Clicking it marks the task as complete — the task title will strike through and the row will dim to indicate completion. Clicking again unchecks the task.
Best practice — how to use checkboxes effectively: Checkboxes are a visual progress tracker for the current session only. For official training documentation, your supervisor or certifier must sign off in the agency training record system. Use the checklist during counseling sessions, self-reviews, or trainer-led walkthroughs to visually track what has been covered.

Personal Property Forms Tab
Located within the Apprentice / Entry tab, the Personal Property Forms block contains all required forms organized into four groups based on their source in DTR Part IV Appendix A-M and supporting chapters.
  • Figures A-M-1 through A-M-8 — Entitlement, Extension & Specialty Worksheets (DD Forms 3151–3158). Use these during counseling to document entitlement extensions, firearms, FRV coverage, pests, home of record/selection, HHG checklist, and food imports.
  • Figures A-M-9 through A-M-16 — Specialty, Storage & PPM Forms (DD Forms 3159–3166). Use for motorcycles, NTS releases and changes, pre-counseling worksheets, POV shipping/storage counseling and authorization, and PPM checklist.
  • Figures A-M-17 through A-M-23 — Authorization, Specialty & Final Counseling Forms (DD Forms 3167–3170, 3178, 1299, 1797). Use for releasing agent authorization, PBP&E, alcoholic beverages, shipper's certification, boat counseling, shipment application, and the final counseling checklist.
  • Additional Required Forms — DD Forms 2278, 1252, 1252-1, 1164, 619, 788, 1780, 1840, 1840R, 1844, and 1162-1. These are used throughout shipment processing, QA inspections, loss/damage reporting, customs, and storage actions.
How to access forms: Each form card links to the DTR Index at business.ustranscom.mil. From the DTR Index, navigate to Appendix A-M or the relevant chapter to download the current fillable PDF version. Always verify you are using the current edition date before use.

Downloading & Printing
Two export options are available at the top of the Civilian Training Task List, next to the title. Use these to save or share the guide.
Download as HTML
Saves a complete, self-contained .html file to your computer. Contains all content, links, and formatting. Open in any browser — no internet connection required. Ideal for saving to a shared drive, emailing to trainees, or using offline on a government network.
Print / Save as PDF
Opens your browser's print dialog with all sections expanded and navigation hidden. Select "Save as PDF" as the printer destination to generate a clean PDF. Ideal for printing physical binders, IG inspection records, or digital filing in official folders.
  • Click Download as HTML in the Training Task List header → a file named Civilian_Training_Task_List_GS2102_GS2130_PersonalProperty_Manning_Ops.html will download automatically.
  • Open the downloaded file in Chrome, Edge, or Firefox for best results. All tabs, checkboxes, and links will work identically to the original.
  • To save as PDF: click Print / Save as PDF → browser print dialog opens → set Destination to "Save as PDF" → adjust margins to "Minimum" for best layout → click Save.
  • For physical binders, print in landscape orientation if the forms grid or chapter index appears cut off.

Trainer & Supervisor Guide
The Trainer Guidance tab provides a complete OJT management framework. It is intended for use by supervisors, section chiefs, and certified trainers responsible for developing civilian employees. Below is how to use it effectively.
Phase 1 — Planning Before Training Begins
Before starting any OJT with a new employee:
1. Confirm your appointment as trainer in writing from the section supervisor.
2. Review the trainee's background, IDP, and existing qualifications.
3. Open the applicable skill level tab (Apprentice/Intermediate/Advanced/Expert) and match task groups to the trainee's duty position.
4. Pull the relevant TTGs from the HAF Traffic Management Portal, DTR chapters via the DTR Index, and DAFI 24-602V4.
5. Build a written training schedule with target completion dates. Share with trainee and supervisor.
Phase 2 — Conducting OJT (Four-Step Method)
For each task, follow the four steps in Trainer Guidance → Phase 2:

Step 1 — Prepare: Brief the trainee on the objective and relevance.
Step 2 — Demonstrate: Perform the task at full pace, then step-by-step while narrating. Reference the applicable DTR chapter and DAFI 24-602V4.
Step 3 — Supervised Practice: Have the trainee perform while you observe. Do not intervene unless safety, data integrity, or customer harm is at risk.
Step 4 — Evaluate & Document: Apply the go/no-go standard. Document completion in the training record system within 5 business days.
Orange Trainer Action Boxes
Throughout the Apprentice, Intermediate, Advanced, and Expert tabs, some tasks contain an Trainer action: callout box. These provide specific trainer instructions for that task — such as how many supervised repetitions are required before certification, what documentation to verify, or what error types to watch for. Read these before attempting to certify the task.
Phase 3 — Documentation Requirements
Every task completion must be documented in the agency training management system (DAF Form 623 equivalent or electronic equivalent):

• Record: task start date, completion date, trainee initials, trainer initials, certifier initials (for core tasks).
• Certifier must be a separately qualified individual from the trainer for core tasks.
• Never backdate entries.
• If a civilian is later found unqualified on a certified task, line through the entry, note the reason, and retrain before recertifying.
Supervisor tip — building the MTL and MTP: Use the Advanced tab → Manning & Ops section as a template for what to include in your Master Task List (MTL) and Master Training Plan (MTP). Every task group in the applicable skill level tabs represents a category for your MTL. Review and update both documents at minimum semi-annually.
