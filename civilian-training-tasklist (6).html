Trainer Guidance — How to Conduct Training with Civilian Technicians
This section provides supervisors, section chiefs, and certified trainers with standardized steps for planning, conducting, and documenting on-the-job training (OJT) for civilian employees across all GS levels.
Phase 1

Planning the Training Program

Before training begins — all levels

▾
Trainer Prerequisites & Preparation
Trainer qualification and appointment
Trainers must be fully qualified to perform the task being taught, appointed in writing by the section supervisor, and have completed any agency-required trainer certification. Certifiers must be at minimum one grade above the trainee or meet equivalent qualification criteria.
OPM Training Policy
Review trainee's background, IDP, and existing qualifications
Before beginning training, review the trainee's prior experience, formal education, completed courses, and current IDP. Adjust training sequence to avoid duplicating competencies already held and to capitalize on existing strengths.
Pull applicable TTGs, CFETP STS, DAFI 24-602V4, and DoW PPA guidance
Obtain current TTGs from the HAF Traffic Management Portal, the applicable STS from the 2T0X1 CFETP, relevant chapters from DAFI 24-602V4, and current DoW PPA advisories and business rules. Map tasks to the trainee's duty position and grade. Identify which tasks require third-party certification.
HAF Traffic Mgmt PortalDAFI 24-602V4DoW PPA
Develop a written Training Schedule with milestones
Create a documented schedule outlining which tasks will be trained, in what sequence, by what method (OJT, formal course, CBT), and by what target completion date. Share with trainee and supervisor. Update quarterly.
Phase 2

Conducting On-the-Job Training (OJT)

Standard four-step OJT method — all tasks

▾
The Four-Step OJT Method
1
Prepare the trainee
Put the trainee at ease. State the task objective and why it matters to the mission. Ask what they already know about the task. Connect new material to prior experience. Outline what they will be able to do by the end of the session.
2
Demonstrate and explain
Perform the task at normal pace first, then at a deliberate step-by-step pace while narrating each action. Reference the TTG, applicable regulation (e.g., DAFI 24-602V4), DoW PPA guidance, and local SOP as you go. Highlight common errors and their consequences. Use actual equipment, systems, and forms wherever possible.
3
Supervised practice
Have the trainee perform the task while you observe. Do not intervene unless safety, data integrity, or customer harm is at risk. Ask the trainee to verbalize their reasoning at key decision points. Allow mistakes when consequences are recoverable — it accelerates learning. Provide immediate corrective feedback after each attempt, not during.
4
Evaluate and document
When the trainee can perform the task accurately, at acceptable speed, without prompting, evaluate against the go/no-go standard. Document completion in the system of record. For core tasks, obtain third-party certifier signature before closing the task.
Go/no-go standard: The trainee can perform the task independently, within local timeliness requirements, with correct use of procedures and documentation — without supervision or reference to a trainer.
Feedback & Coaching Best Practices
Provide structured, specific feedback after each training session
Use a consistent feedback format: (1) what was done correctly, (2) what needs improvement and why, (3) specific corrective action for next session. Written feedback is preferred for complex tasks. Avoid vague praise without specifics.
OPM Performance
Conduct initial work center orientation and review of trainee's training records
On the trainee's first day in the section, review their training record folder together. Confirm what tasks are open, in progress, or completed. Clarify the certification process, expectations, and your availability for questions.
Utilize training systems to monitor individual training progress
Maintain an up-to-date view of each trainee's task completion status in the agency training management system. Flag trainees not progressing at expected pace. Conduct monthly training progress reviews and document results.
Phase 3

Training Documentation & Certification

Required records for all civilian employees

▾
Records Requirements
Establish and maintain Training Record Folder (DAF Form 623 equivalent)
Every civilian employee requires a training record documenting all OJT qualifications, course completions, and certifications. Maintain records per agency Records Management program. Records must be accessible for supervisory review and IG inspections.
NARA
Document task start date, completion date, trainee initials, trainer initials, and certifier initials
For each task in the STS or MTL: record training start, completion, and all required signatures. For core tasks, the certifier must be a separately qualified individual (not the same person as the trainer). Never backdate entries.
Process decertification and recertification when required
If a civilian is found unqualified on a previously certified task (e.g., after an IG finding or extended absence), line through or delete the certification entry and note the reason. Retrain and recertify before the task is re-entered as current.
Input training requirements to the appropriate system of record
Maintain task qualification records in the agency-designated training management system. Ensure records accurately reflect current qualifications at all times. Update within 5 business days of task completion.

DAFI 24-602V4DoW PPA
Develop and maintain Job Safety Training Outline (JSTO)
Maintain a current JSTO for all duty positions in the section. Update when equipment, procedures, or safety standards change. Ensure all personnel complete annual safety training and that completion is documented.
OSHA Training
Review training program to assess overall effectiveness
Annually, evaluate training outcomes by measuring error rates, QA inspection findings, and customer complaint rates against section performance targets. Adjust training content, frequency, or delivery methods based on data.
